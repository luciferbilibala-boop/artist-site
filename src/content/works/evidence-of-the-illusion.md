## 策展前言 / Curatorial Statement

### 中文

20 世纪初，当洛夫克拉夫特（H.P. Lovecraft）首次将目光投向浩瀚而冷酷的宇宙时，他给人类文明敲响了第一声丧钟：人类引以为傲的智能、道德与尊严，不过是广袤虚无中如幻觉般瞬间生灭的微光。这种“宇宙主义恐惧（Cosmicism）”在当时曾是一份震慑心灵的哲学寓言。

然而在今天，这种恐怖不再是远古神话，它已完全降临为我们的日常。

当下的我们正处在一个空前的“Neo-Cthulhu（新克苏鲁）”时代：AI 的飞速进化与黑盒化，正在以前所未有的速度剥离人类对“智慧”与“创造力”的垄断，甚至开始反向定义人类的存在；深空航天的狂暴探索，将脆弱的肉体推向无声、冰冷、无边无际的射线深渊；基因工程与延寿技术，则将生命解构为可被改写、剪辑与计算的代码。人类的自我认知，正经历着一场从“万物之灵”跌落为“尘埃标本”的剧烈坍塌。

放到宇宙的时空尺度上，人类文明的繁盛与衰亡都变得微不足道。宇宙最深沉的漠然，正在于这一切的发生与消失都无须得到回应。也正是在这个视角下，文明之间的无知和傲慢变得可见。一个文明面对另一个陌生文明时，只能用自己已知的框架去解释，就像人类为新发现的海洋生物和天体命名。命名让未知变得可以理解，对未知的好奇与把自己的经验当作普遍尺度的傲慢，常常就发生在同一个动作里。

Hydon BAI 的独幅版画（Monotype）《幻觉的证据》系列呈现了我们此刻正在经历的这两种冲动：不断向未知探去，也不断试图用熟悉的尺度安置它。克苏鲁文明中恐怖的未知生物如我们眼中熟悉的海产，而我们引以为傲的文明又是不是另一个文明眼中的小鱼干？十字架是宗教还是路标？鱼是食物还是门铃？这些来自另一个视角的疑问，被艺术家以自己独创的独幅版画呈现，每一幅作品都是一次无法复制的压印，记录着我们熟悉的秩序，也让我们看见：我们对文明伟大、主体性稳固的确信，可能本身就是一场幻觉。我们如何以自身的无知去定义他者，也必被如此对待。但每一个文明都是如此，宇宙亦是如此。

### English

In the early 20th century, when H.P. Lovecraft first cast his gaze into the vast, indifferent cosmos, he  sounded the first knell for human civilization: human intelligence, morality, and dignity are but  fleeting illusions born and extinguished in an endless void. This "Cosmicism" was, at the time, a  haunting philosophical parable.

Today, however, this horror is no longer an ancient myth; it has fully materialized as our lived,  everyday reality.

We inhabit an unprecedented "Neo-Cthulhu" era. The rapid evolution and black-boxing of artificial  intelligence are stripping humanity of its monopoly over wisdom and creativity, going so far as to  re-define human existence itself. Deep-space exploration thrusts fragile flesh into an abyssal silence  swept by cosmic rays. Meanwhile, genetic engineering and life-extension technologies deconstruct  life into rewritable, editable, and computable lines of code. Human self-perception is collapsing  dramatically—from the "crown of creation" down to "specimens in the dust."

On the scale of cosmic time and space, the flourishing and decline of human civilization become negligible. The deepest indifference of the universe lies in the fact that all of this can come into being and disappear without calling for a response. From this vantage point, the ignorance and arrogance that mark encounters between civilizations become visible. A civilization can interpret an unfamiliar one only through the frameworks it already knows, just as humans name newly discovered marine species and celestial bodies. Naming makes the unknown intelligible. Curiosity about the unknown and the arrogance of taking one’s own experience as a universal measure often belong to the very same act.

Hydon BAI’s monotype series Evidence of the Illusion gives form to two impulses we are living through now: the urge to venture into the unknown and the urge to fit it into familiar frames of reference. What appears in the world of Cthulhu as a terrifying, unknown creature resembles what we know simply as seafood. Might the civilization we are so proud of look like a little dried fish to another civilization? Is a cross a religious symbol or a signpost? Is a fish food or a doorbell? The artist gives form to these questions, which arise from another point of view, through a monotype process of his own devising. Each work is an impression that cannot be replicated. It records the order we know and allows us to see that our certainty about the greatness of civilization and the stability of our subjectivity may itself be an illusion. Just as we define others through our own ignorance, we too will be defined in this way. Yet so it is with every civilization, and with the universe itself.
